Chapter 5: Causal Inference and Randomized Controlled Trials

1 Outline

This chapter introduces potential outcomes, the average treatment effect (ATE), randomized controlled trials, the difference in means, and regression adjustment. The code cells run in your browser through WebR; no local R installation or remote R session is required.

2 1. Potential outcomes

For individual \(i\), let \(D_i\in\{0,1\}\) denote treatment and let \(Y_i(d)\) denote the outcome that would be observed under treatment status \(d\). The observed outcome obeys consistency:

\[ Y_i=Y_i(D_i)=D_iY_i(1)+(1-D_i)Y_i(0). \]

This notation also presumes a well-defined treatment and no interference between individuals. Together these conditions are usually grouped under SUTVA.

The individual effect \(Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\) is normally unobservable because we see only one potential outcome for each person. This is the fundamental problem of causal inference. We therefore begin with

\[ \tau=\operatorname{ATE} =\mathbb E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)]. \]

3 2. Randomization and identification

In a simple randomized experiment,

\[ D_i\perp\!\!\!\perp (Y_i(1),Y_i(0)), \qquad \Pr(D_i=1)=\pi\in(0,1). \]

Randomization and consistency imply

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau &=\mathbb E[Y_i(1)]-\mathbb E[Y_i(0)]\\ &=\mathbb E[Y_i(1)\mid D_i=1]-\mathbb E[Y_i(0)\mid D_i=0]\\ &=\mathbb E[Y_i\mid D_i=1]-\mathbb E[Y_i\mid D_i=0]. \end{aligned} \]

The last line identifies a causal parameter using the distribution of observed variables.

4 3. Difference in means

The sample analogue is

\[ \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{DM}} =\frac{1}{n_1}\sum_{i:D_i=1}Y_i -\frac{1}{n_0}\sum_{i:D_i=0}Y_i. \]

It is exactly the OLS slope from a regression of \(Y\) on an intercept and \(D\). Under Bernoulli assignment,

\[ \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{DM}}-\tau) \overset{d}{\longrightarrow} N\!\left(0, \frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i(1))}{\pi} +\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i(0))}{1-\pi} \right). \]

Use a heteroskedasticity-robust standard error because the two treatment arms need not have the same outcome variance.

4.1 Survey-wording experiment

Respondents in a simplified General Social Survey experiment were randomly asked whether the government spends too much on either “welfare” (\(D=1\)) or “assistance to the poor” (\(D=0\)). The outcome equals one for a “yes” response.

The direct difference and the coefficient on D are numerically identical.

5 4. Regression adjustment

Let \(W_i\) contain pre-treatment covariates and suppose assignment is independent of potential outcomes and these covariates:

\[ D_i\perp\!\!\!\perp (Y_i(1),Y_i(0),W_i). \]

Adjustment is unnecessary for identification but can improve precision. A robust default centers the covariates and allows their slopes to differ by arm:

\[ Y_i=\alpha+\tau D_i+(W_i-\bar W)'\beta_0 +D_i(W_i-\bar W)'(\beta_1-\beta_0)+u_i. \]

The coefficient on \(D_i\) compares the fitted treatment and control outcomes at the overall covariate mean. It consistently estimates the ATE even when the fitted linear outcome regressions are not the true conditional means. It need not be exactly unbiased in a finite sample, so use a robust standard error and understand the guarantee as asymptotic.

5.1 Why interactions matter for the variance argument

Let \(X_i=W_i-\mathbb E[W_i]\), \(\Sigma=\mathbb E[X_iX_i']\), and define the separate arm slopes

\[ \gamma_d=\Sigma^{-1} \mathbb E\!\left[X_i\{Y_i(d)-\mathbb E[Y_i(d)]\}\right]. \]

For \(r_i(d)=Y_i(d)-\mathbb E[Y_i(d)]-X_i'\gamma_d\), the projection normal equations imply

\[ \operatorname{Var}(Y_i(d)) =\operatorname{Var}(r_i(d))+\gamma_d'\Sigma\gamma_d. \]

Because the adjusted contrast is evaluated at the overall sample covariate mean, sampling variation in that mean also matters when the two arm-specific slopes differ. The fully interacted estimator has asymptotic variance

\[ V_{\mathrm{RA}} =\frac{\operatorname{Var}(r_i(1))}{\pi} +\frac{\operatorname{Var}(r_i(0))}{1-\pi} +(\gamma_1-\gamma_0)'\Sigma(\gamma_1-\gamma_0), \]

and therefore

\[ V_{\mathrm{DM}}-V_{\mathrm{RA}} =\left\{ \sqrt{\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}}\gamma_1 +\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}}\gamma_0 \right\}' \Sigma \left\{ \sqrt{\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}}\gamma_1 +\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}}\gamma_0 \right\} \geq0. \]

This proof does not assume that a covariance conditional on \(D=1\) equals the corresponding covariance conditional on \(D=0\). An un-interacted adjustment remains consistent under randomization, but with unequal assignment probabilities and different arm-specific slopes it lacks the same general no-loss-of-precision guarantee.

Only pre-treatment covariates belong in this adjustment. Conditioning on a variable caused by treatment can change the estimand and introduce bias.

6 Summary

  • Randomization identifies the ATE as a difference in observable means.
  • The difference in means is exactly the treatment coefficient in the short OLS regression.
  • Centered, fully interacted regression adjustment can improve precision without requiring the linear working models to be correct.
  • SUTVA, randomized treatment, appropriate moments, and a nonsingular covariate second-moment matrix remain substantive requirements.
